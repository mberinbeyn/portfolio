Creating a truly engaging experience for English learners

Product Design UX Research EdTech User Testing Audio UX

Overview

I led research and design for the new "Books" product at Puzzle English—from concept to launch and beyond. The product helps boost engagement by offering an immersive way to learn English through classic literature.

When I joined the project, the CPO envisioned a dedicated section within the Puzzle English platform focused on books. The core features included side-by-side translation and authentic audio narration by professional actors or famous personalities, such as Stephen Fry narrating Harry Potter.

Goal & Objectives

My key challenge was to design an intuitive and engaging user experience within a tight timeline, applying my expertise in user research, UX/UI design, and audio integration to create a seamless learning tool that aligned with Puzzle English's strategic objectives.

Competitive Analysis

First, I researched similar products on the market and read what users were saying about them. I wanted to understand their needs better, so I looked at their reviews and feedback to find common pain points and missing features. This helped me define priorities and focus on the most important tasks for the MVP.

User Profiles and Journey

I described the main types of users and how they usually interact with the product. Below is one of the user stories:

Interaction Concept

Eva learns about a new product from the Puzzle English newsletter and goes to the page with the product description. She reads the information and proceeds to the product. She studies the catalog of books in the library, selects an optimal one for herself based on her proficiency level and favorite genre "Adventure." Among the presented books, she chooses the familiar one "Alice in Wonderland." On the description page, she views detailed information, looks at user comments to understand whether it is worth reading or to check another book. She opens the book. While listening to the narrator, she follows the text, looks up translations of unfamiliar words, and adds them to the vocabulary for studying later.

UX/UI Design

To launch the product quickly, I reused existing elements and pages whenever possible. We started with the desktop version, and later created a mobile version to test the demand for the "Books" feature.

Collecting and Analyzing User Feedback

After launching the product, I asked the support team to send me all feedback from the first users. I organized this feedback in one file, which helped me understand users' problems better and find areas where the product could be improved.

Structure feedback clearly to identify the most effective improvements

Hypothesis and Prototype for Testing

From session recordings, I noticed that users spent a long time browsing the catalog, and some left the page without choosing a book. To keep users engaged and help them find books faster, I suggested adding popular preset filters for selecting books.

Quick filters for faster book discovery and better user retention

Results

In the first month after launch, more than 30 users wrote positive comments about the new audio reading feature. They said it helped them learn better. These comments were not from a survey — users shared their feedback by themselves.
